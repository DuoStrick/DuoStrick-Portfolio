Most local video players on Android show you a list of filenames. You scroll, you squint at `Show.Name.S03E04.1080p.mkv`, you tap, and if you stopped halfway through last night you get to hunt for your place all over again.

**Mediqora** does something different. It *reads* those filenames. A folder of downloads becomes Show Name → Season 3 → Episode 4, with your progress tracked per episode and the next one waiting on the home screen.

It's out now on Google Play, it's free, and it has no internet permission at all.

## A Library, Not a File Browser

This is the part that makes Mediqora worth installing over whatever player came with your phone.

Drop in a season of episodes and Mediqora parses the filenames to work out the series, the season, and the episode number. What you get back is a browsable library:

- **Automatic series and episode detection** straight from filenames
- **Resume every video and song** exactly where you left off
- **Up Next** — the next unwatched episode is one tap from the home screen
- **Autoplay the next episode**, or the next file in the folder, when one ends
- **Recently added**, favorites, and your own collections
- **Browse by series, video, music, or folder** — whichever matches how you think
- **Fast search** across titles, series, folders and artists, indexed on your device

The same logic works for anything numbered in sequence: TV shows, lecture recordings, podcast archives, a course you downloaded. If the filenames carry the structure, Mediqora finds it.

## A Video Player That Gets Out of the Way

Once something is playing, the interface should disappear. Mediqora's player is built around gestures and quick controls:

- **Swipe to seek**, change volume, and change brightness
- **Skip 10 seconds** forward or back
- **Playback speed from 0.5× to 2×** — useful for lectures and podcasts
- **Fit, fill or stretch** the picture to your screen, or pinch to switch between them
- **Screen lock** so a stray touch doesn't interrupt playback
- **Picture-in-picture** — keep watching while you use other apps
- **Subtitle and audio track selection**, including external SRT and VTT files
- **Volume boost** for quiet recordings

The volume boost is worth calling out. Older recordings and phone-captured video are often mastered far too quietly for a phone speaker, and boosting past the system maximum is usually the difference between "unwatchable on the train" and fine.

## It Handles Your Music Too

Mediqora isn't a video player with an audio afterthought bolted on — the same library and resume logic covers your music:

- **Background playback** with lock screen and headset controls
- **Play queue** — add tracks, reorder them, play next
- **Album art, artist and album details**

Because resume is per-item, a long podcast episode or audiobook chapter picks up exactly where you left it, the same way a video does.

## Private by Design, Not by Promise

Plenty of apps say they respect your privacy. Mediqora's version of that claim is structural: **the app does not request internet access**, so nothing you watch, search or listen to is able to leave your device.

That means:

- No account to create
- No ads
- No analytics, no tracking
- Your library, watch history and favorites stay on the phone

This isn't a setting you have to find and switch on. The permission simply isn't in the app, so there is no code path that could send your viewing habits anywhere.

On the permissions side, Mediqora asks only for access to the videos and music on your device, so it can build the library. It does not ask for contacts, location, camera, or microphone.

## Works With Your Files

Mediqora plays the common video and audio formats your device supports, including **MP4, MKV, WebM, MP3, AAC and FLAC**. Beyond browsing your own library, you can:

- Open a file from your file manager or any other app via **"Open with"**
- Add folders from an **SD card or any location** using Android's own folder picker

## Getting the Most Out of Series Detection

Series detection is only as good as the filenames you give it. A few things that help:

1. **Keep the `SxxExx` pattern** if it's already there — `S03E04`, `s03e04` and similar forms are what the parser looks for first.
2. **Keep episodes of one show together** in a folder. Folder structure gives the parser extra signal when a filename is ambiguous.
3. **Don't over-clean your filenames.** Stripping a name down to `04.mkv` removes the information Mediqora uses to group it.
4. **Use Up Next.** Once a series is grouped, you rarely need to open the library at all — the home screen already knows what you're watching.

## Who Mediqora Is For

Mediqora is built for people who keep their media on their own phone and want it organised, remembered and played well — without handing it to anyone else. If your storage is full of downloaded shows, recorded lectures, or a music collection you actually own, this is the player that treats it like a library instead of a directory listing.

It's free on Google Play, works entirely offline, and has no ads.
