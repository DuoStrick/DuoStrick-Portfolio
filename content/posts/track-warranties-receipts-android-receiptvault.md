The washing machine stops spinning. You're fairly sure it's still under warranty — but the card is somewhere in a drawer, the invoice is buried in an email from last year, and you can't remember whether it was a one-year or two-year cover. So you pay for the repair.

Almost everyone has done this. The fix isn't a better drawer; it's a system that remembers expiry dates for you.

## What Actually Needs Tracking

More than phones and laptops. Anything with a receipt or an end date:

- **Electronics** — phones, laptops, tablets, TVs, earphones, smartwatches
- **Home appliances** — AC, fridge, washing machine, geyser, microwave
- **Vehicles** — service due dates, insurance, PUC renewal
- **AMC contracts** — water purifier, AC servicing, lift or generator maintenance
- **Insurance and memberships** — health policies, gym and club memberships
- **Documents** — passport and driving-licence renewals
- **Online orders** — the invoice is often the only proof of purchase

## The Four Details That Matter at Claim Time

When something breaks, the service centre usually asks for:

1. **Purchase date** — to check the warranty is still running
2. **Proof of purchase** — the bill or invoice
3. **Model and serial number**
4. **Warranty period** — including any extended cover you bought

Capture these the day you buy something and a claim takes minutes.

## A Simple Routine

- **Photograph the bill at the till** or save the invoice as soon as the order arrives — thermal receipts fade within months.
- **Record the warranty length**, not just the purchase date.
- **Set a reminder a month before expiry.** That's your window to report any fault while it's still covered.
- **Keep the box serial sticker** in the same record — it's often the first thing support asks for.
